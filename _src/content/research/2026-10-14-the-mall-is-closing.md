## Prologue: Saturday, October 2028

Yuna never searched for the jacket. She didn’t know she wanted it.

On Tuesday she saved a reel of a creator walking through Kyoto’s
backstreets in a waxed olive field coat. On Wednesday she told her agent
she was flying from Singapore to Hokkaido in November. On Thursday she
complained, to nobody in particular, that her old parka made her look
like a sleeping bag.

On Saturday morning, between the coffee and the shower, a message sat
waiting in WhatsApp.

*“Sapporo will be 1 to 8°C, with early snow. I found three coats close
to the one you saved. The original is \$640 from the brand’s own site. A
family workshop in Yorkshire makes one in the same waxed cotton for
\$215, ships in five days, 4.8 stars from 1,900 buyers. A third is \$89
but the reviews say the wax cracks. I’d take the Yorkshire one. Want
it?”*

She tapped yes. Her saved card, her saved address, done. She never saw a
search bar. She never saw a sponsored listing. She never opened the
everything store.

The Yorkshire workshop has eleven employees. It runs on Shopify. It has
never bought a banner ad. It was found because its product was good and
its price was fair, and because an agent that knew Yuna had read every
corner of the internet on her behalf.

Nobody went to the mall.

This piece is about who gets paid in Yuna’s Saturday morning. My answer:
the rails get paid every time (Shopify), the assistant who knows her
gets paid on commission (Meta), and the mall has to reinvent itself or
shrink (Amazon).

## 1. The rails: Shopify wins whichever agent wins

Shopify does not need Meta to win. It needs agents to win. That is a
much easier bet.

In Yuna’s story the agent did the finding. But someone still had to hold
the catalog, run the checkout, charge the card, calculate Singapore’s
import GST, book the courier and handle the return. That someone was
Shopify. It earns the same cut whether the buyer arrived from Muse,
ChatGPT, Google or a bookmark.

Management has said this almost word for word. CFO Jeff Hoffmeister, at
[Goldman Sachs Communacopia on 10 September
2026](https://web.quartr.com/companies/4014?companyId=4014&documentId=4162880&documentType=transcript&eventId=745426&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=2145.58):

> “Whether you get to the merchant’s website, which Shopify is running,
> whether you get to that website by coming through Muse or coming
> through OpenAI or going straight to the merchant’s website or you did
> a Google search or whatever it is, all those are channels that lead
> back to the merchant.”

Head of Growth Archie Abrams, [same
session](https://web.quartr.com/companies/4014?companyId=4014&documentId=4162880&documentType=transcript&eventId=745426&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=812.39):
*“As the fragmentation gets even more over time, all roads lead back to
Shopify.”*

And on economics, President Harley Finkelstein on the [Q2-26
call](https://web.quartr.com/companies/4014?companyId=4014&documentId=3968357&documentType=transcript&eventId=660732&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=1944.48):
*“Agentic transactions carry the exact same economics as an online store
transaction. There’s no new fees.”* More agent traffic means more GMV at
Shopify’s existing take rate of about 3.1%.

**The early numbers point the right way.** From the [Q2-26
call](https://web.quartr.com/companies/4014?companyId=4014&documentId=3968357&documentType=transcript&eventId=660732&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=3228.4):

- Traffic to Shopify stores from AI agents: **3x** year on year. Orders:
  **3x**.
- New buyers from AI channels: **2x** the rate of other channels.
- **75%** of AI-attributed purchases came from outside the top 100
  product categories.

That last number is the whole thesis in one line. Agents are finding the
long tail. The Yorkshire workshop is not a fantasy. It is three-quarters
of the agent orders Shopify already sees.

**The Meta deal turns the tap on wider.** In the week of 21 September
2026 Shopify and Meta announced that Muse, Meta’s personal agent, can
search the full Shopify catalog and pay with Shop Pay. Zuckerberg’s
post: *“Shoppers find more. Shops sell more.”* He added: *“More
partnerships like this coming soon”*
([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/09/61911829/meta-shopify-ai-shopping-partnership-zuckerberg)).
Alexandr Wang, Meta’s Chief AI Officer, at [Connect on 23
September](https://web.quartr.com/companies/4109?companyId=4109&documentId=4199046&documentType=transcript&eventId=759230&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=598.18):
*“Muse can access the entire Shopify product catalog, agentically.”*

There is a historical irony here. Meta spent five years trying to own
checkout inside Facebook and Instagram Shops, then shut native checkout
down in 2025 and sent buyers back to merchant websites ([PPC
Land](https://ppc.land/meta-phases-out-facebook-and-instagram-shops-checkout-by-august-2025/)).
This time Meta isn’t rebuilding the till. It is renting Shopify’s.

**The honest caveat.** Finkelstein himself called agent volume *“small
relative to \$116 billion GMV.”* Shopify has never disclosed agentic
GMV. Today this is a trend, not yet a revenue line.

## 2. The shop assistant on commission: what advertising becomes

Meta is an advertising company today, and its ad business is changing
shape. In Q2-26
([10-Q](https://web.quartr.com/companies/4109?companyId=4109&documentId=3669153&documentType=report&eventId=657318&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=42)):

- **Advertising: \$59.36bn, 97.6% of revenue, +27%**
- Other revenue: \$1.01bn, 1.7%, +73%. Mostly businesses paying to
  message customers on WhatsApp, plus subscriptions.
- Reality Labs: \$0.43bn, 0.7%.

One sentence in that filing matters more than the totals: *“the online
commerce vertical was the largest contributor to the increase in
advertising revenue.”* Meta already makes its commerce money. The
question is what form it takes next.

**So what is advertising, really?** It has never been one thing.

1.  First it was a banner beside the content.
2.  Then it was a post inside the feed, dressed like content.
3.  Then it was the content itself: a creator holding a lipstick, paid
    by the brand.
4.  Next, I think, it is a recommendation from an assistant who knows
    you.

Think of the best sales assistant in a department store. She takes
commission from ten brands. She could push whichever one pays most, but
she would not last a season. The good ones match the client to the right
product, earn the commission anyway, and keep the client for twenty
years. Her honesty is the business model, not a cost to it.

Muse can be that assistant at the scale of 3.6 billion people.

**And the payment changes with it.** A banner is bought on a fixed
budget and judged on clicks. An assistant’s work is measured exactly:
Muse recommended it, Yuna bought it, \$215. So the brand can pay a share
of the sales Muse actually made. That is pay-for-value, not
pay-for-exposure. It slots straight into a brand’s marketing budget
because it *is* the marketing budget, only spent after the sale instead
of before it.

This is already the stated plan, if not yet the stated price. Zuckerberg
told Alex Heath on 8 September that Muse will be funded long-term by “a
very small cut” of commerce transactions, paid by merchants
([RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/meta-muse-ai-agent-transaction-fees-free-tokens)).
The rate is undisclosed. For scale, OpenAI charges merchants **4%** on
ChatGPT checkout sales
([Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/shopify-confirms-a-4-fee-on-sales-made-through-chatgpt-but-whats-the-real-cost/)).
Every \$100bn of agent-routed sales at 3% is \$3bn of revenue that looks
nothing like a banner.

It will take years to matter against a \$237bn annual ad run-rate. That
is fine. The commission does not replace the ad budget on day one. It is
where the ad budget migrates to.

**The hire that says it out loud.** On Monday 28 September 2026, MongoDB
announced that CEO CJ Desai had stepped down “effective immediately” to
take “a senior role at Meta Platforms” ([MongoDB
release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mongodb-announces-ceo-transition-302891360.html)).
He had been CEO for less than a year. MongoDB’s Investor Day was the
next morning. Its stock fell about 25% and Meta’s slipped about 4%
([Yahoo
Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/meta-enterprise-ai-platform-mongodb-144812312.html)).

The title matters: **Chief Enterprise Platform Officer, reporting
directly to Zuckerberg.** His mandate is to build Meta’s Enterprise
Platform, starting with Muse, a business agent tool and a coding app.
Desai is an enterprise seller by training. His career runs through
Oracle, EMC, Symantec, ServiceNow (where he rose to president) and
Cloudflare (president of product and engineering), then MongoDB.

You don’t take a sitting public-company CEO out of his chair, the day
before his own investor day, to run a consumer chatbot. You do it to
sign up businesses. My read: Desai’s job is to line up brands and
merchants that pay Muse to sell for them. That is the commission-based
sales assistant above, with an enterprise sales force behind it.
Zuckerberg’s own framing points the same way: Meta will use “advanced
models, leading agents, large-scale infrastructure, and years of working
closely with many businesses.” The mandate as reported also covers
coding tools and the API, so commerce is one use of the hire, not the
only one.

**My hypothesis: users will opt in to being known.** Today Meta keeps a
firewall. Muse “doesn’t share people’s conversations or data with Meta’s
ads systems”
([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/meta-debuts-its-muse-ai-agent-will-consumers-trust-it/)).
I don’t think that is the end state. I can foresee users being offered a
choice: *let Muse use what you like on Instagram to shop for you.*
Speaking as someone who grew up with Chinese apps, I would tick yes
without thinking. Many younger users would too. Not everyone will, and
that is the point of a choice. But the users who opt in get Yuna’s
Saturday morning, and the users who don’t get a search box.

**The engine and the fuel.** Muse needs two things to keep getting
better: compute (the energy) and data (the food). Meta has both in
unusual quantity. On compute, Zuckerberg on the [Q2-26
call](https://web.quartr.com/companies/4109?companyId=4109&documentId=3925041&documentType=transcript&eventId=657318&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=593.34):
*“We’re getting a lot of offers for compute at a significant premium
over what we paid for it.”* He is choosing to keep most of it for his
own agents. That choice tells you how much he thinks Muse is worth.

## 3. The mall: Amazon won’t wait to be bypassed

Picture a brand manager in Seattle in 2024. Her rival’s rain jacket sits
at the top of the search page. She bids \$2.40 a click to put hers right
beside it. Millions of shoppers scroll past both. That shelf-space
auction is one of the best businesses ever built.

It rests on one assumption: a human has to walk the aisles. Yuna’s agent
doesn’t walk aisles. It doesn’t need a mall at all. A tucked-away
workshop in Yorkshire is as easy to reach as the everything store. It is
all about the product.

**The shelf is worth more than the store.** Amazon’s Q2-26 numbers
([Q2-26
release](https://web.quartr.com/companies/4762?companyId=4762&documentId=3669863&documentType=report&eventId=657334&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=9),
[call](https://web.quartr.com/companies/4762?companyId=4762&documentId=3950634&documentType=transcript&eventId=657334&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=1026.7)):

- Advertising: **\$19.8bn, +26%**. That is about \$79bn a year.
- North America retail operating income: \$9.1bn. International:
  \$1.7bn. Together about \$43bn a year.
- AWS operating income: **\$16.6bn**, 60% of the group’s \$27.5bn.

Read those together. Amazon’s ad revenue is bigger than the operating
profit of its entire retail business. The ads are the profit engine of
the mall. That is exactly the part an agent walks past.

**So Amazon shut the door.** About two weeks after Muse launched on 8
September, Amazon blocked it. The message to users: *“Continued access
by an unauthorized AI agent violates Amazon’s Conditions of Use”*
([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/amazon-blocks-metas-muse-ai-assistant-in-new-standoff-over-agentic-shopping/)).
The same Monday, Muse hit \#1 in the free app charts and Meta closed up
11%
([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/21/amazon-meta-muse-ai-agentic-shopping)).
The market had picked a narrative.

I read the block as buying time, not as a strategy. And I don’t think
Amazon will sit still. It never has.

**Amazon already holds most of the ingredients.**

- **The data.** It knows what sold, at what price, from which seller, to
  whom, and what came back.
- **The logistics.** Prime delivery and returns, which no agent can
  replace.
- **The compute.** Its own chips (Trainium) now run at **over \$25bn a
  year, growing triple digits** ([Q2-26
  call](https://web.quartr.com/companies/4762?companyId=4762&documentId=3950634&documentType=transcript&eventId=657334&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=200.08)),
  with over \$225bn of committed Trainium revenue ([Q1-26
  call](https://web.quartr.com/companies/4762?companyId=4762&documentId=3272550&documentType=transcript&eventId=552741&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=488.04)).
  Agents run on inference, and inference is exactly what Trainium sells.
- **An agent, already in use.** Alexa for Shopping (formerly Rufus)
  reached **350m customers** in 12 months. Active users nearly doubled
  in Q2 and interactions rose 5x. Shoppers who click a sponsored prompt
  inside it convert 48% more often and spend 21% more.

The missing piece is an agent that lives where people actually spend
their day, not inside the mall’s own app. Muse lives in WhatsApp. Alexa
for Shopping lives behind Amazon’s front door. Thread data, logistics,
compute and ads together through one personal agent that travels with
the customer, and the mall becomes the best-stocked assistant on the
internet. One acquisition or one partnership could do it. If it comes, I
expect the stock to re-rate fast, because the story would finally make
sense.

**The obvious target: Instinct.** The hottest independent personal agent
right now is [Instinct](https://instinct.co/), built by 23-year-old Noah
Shinn, a former Sierra engineer and co-author of the Reflexion agent
paper. You text it or call it. It learns what you like, then books, buys
and follows up for you. Per
[Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/30/noah-shinn-instinct-ai-assistant-meta-muse-alexandr-wang-tech-series-c-ai-agent-mark-zuckerberg/)
(30 September 2026):

- Invite-only since August 2026. Invitations have resold on eBay. Zero
  marketing spend.
- Series B: \$250m at a \$2.5bn valuation (August). Series C: **\$1bn at
  a \$10bn valuation** (September). Named investors include Sequoia,
  Benchmark and Coatue.
- Approaching **\$1bn of annual transaction volume**, about half of it
  travel.
- On 5 October it moved into group chats, including friends without an
  account
  ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/instinct-brings-its-ai-agent-to-group-chats-even-for-friends-without-an-account/)).

Meta’s Alexandr Wang has already mocked it in public, which tells you
who Meta thinks the rival is. Muse had 2.8m installs in its first 12
days.

Picture Instinct with Amazon’s catalog, Prime delivery and Trainium
behind it. That is the threaded-together agent this thesis is waiting
for. A \$10bn price is 0.4% of Amazon’s \$2.76tn market cap. Money is
not the obstacle.

**The obstacles are real, though.**

- **Antitrust.** Amazon walked away from iRobot in 2024 under EU and FTC
  pressure. Buying the leading independent shopping agent would draw the
  same fire.
- **Amazon’s own playbook is the acqui-hire.** In 2024 it hired Adept’s
  founders and licensed the technology rather than buying the company
  ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/28/amazon-hires-founders-away-from-ai-startup-adept)).
  I’d expect a licence or partnership before a full takeover.
- **Neutrality is the asset.** Instinct’s users trust it because it
  shops everywhere. Under Amazon it becomes the mall’s own salesperson,
  which is the exact thing my thesis says users are leaving.
- **Price.** Investors who paid \$10bn three weeks ago will want a large
  premium.

The Instinct deal itself is optional. What matters is Amazon getting a
personal agent people already live in. A partnership with Instinct, or
an equivalent, is the catalyst I’m watching for.

**Where I push back on my own thesis**

1.  **Amazon’s agent is not a failure on the numbers.** 350m users and
    5x interactions is real traction. The problem is where it lives, not
    whether it works.
2.  **An honest agent may still pick Amazon.** If Yuna needed the coat
    by Friday, Prime wins. The real risk is not lost sales. It is Amazon
    being turned into a warehouse that agents order from without ever
    seeing an ad. Amazon itself reportedly frames the danger exactly
    that way
    ([Acadia](https://acadia.io/retail-roundup-september-2026)).
3.  **Buying AMZN is mostly buying AWS.** AWS is 60% of operating income
    and growing 37%. Every agent, Muse included, needs inference
    somewhere. Agentic commerce threatens Amazon’s ad shelf, but the
    agent boom feeds its cloud.
4.  **The stock is not a laggard.** AMZN is +11.3% YTD at \$256.81, but
    10.6% below its 52-week high of \$287.20. It has pulled back, not
    broken down.

## 4. Valuation: Shopify prices in the future, Amazon barely does

Shopify already trades as if the agent future is arriving. Amazon trades
as if it is a threat. Multiples at the 6 October 2026 close
([stockanalysis.com](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/shop/statistics/)
statistics and consensus pages):

| Company      | EV/Sales (TTM) | Forward P/E | 2026E revenue growth | 2026E EPS growth |
|--------------|----------------|-------------|----------------------|------------------|
| Shopify      | 15.6x          | 78.7x       | 31.9%                | 33.6%            |
| MercadoLibre | 2.9x           | 44.0x       | 44.5%                | -2.0%            |
| Meta         | 8.3x           | 23.0x       | 26.5%                | 3.8%             |
| Amazon       | 3.7x           | 27.3x       | 15.5%                | 15.9%            |

**Shopify (\$164.68, market cap ~\$212bn, net cash \$5.3bn).**

- 86x 2026E EPS (\$1.91) and 67x 2027E (\$2.45). EV/2027E sales: 10.8x.
- Forward P/E divided by EPS growth is about 2.3x. Growth investors
  usually pay 1.5 to 2x. This is a premium for quality and optionality.
- GAAP P/E (111x) is unreliable because net income swings with marks on
  equity stakes. Operating income and FCF are cleaner: 115x TTM
  operating income (\$1.84bn).
- The 54-analyst consensus target is \$172.77, 4.9% above today. The
  Street already agrees with the story.
- The stock is 9.6% below its \$182.19 high, not at a high. The 13-week
  low is \$111.06.

**Amazon (\$256.81, market cap ~\$2.76tn).**

- 27x forward P/E and 3.7x EV/sales. EV/TTM operating income (\$93.7bn)
  is about 31x.
- TTM GAAP P/E of 20.6x flatters it. Q2-26 included \$53.4bn of
  non-operating income against \$27.5bn of operating income.
- PEG of 1.3x. Consensus target \$330.59, 29% above today from 59
  analysts.
- 13-week low \$226.16. 26-week low \$219.62.

**Sense check.** MercadoLibre looks cheap on sales because it books
first-party retail and lending revenue. It is not a clean comparison on
that line, only on growth. On the cleaner lines, Shopify costs roughly
3x Amazon’s forward P/E for roughly 2x the growth. That premium is the
price of being the neutral rails. Amazon, at 27x for a business whose
cloud is growing 37%, prices in very little agent upside and a good deal
of agent fear. That asymmetry is why I want both: Shopify on a pullback,
Amazon on the bet that it moves first.

## 5. Company snapshots

All three are still run or controlled by founders or founder-era
leaders. Revenue mix is Q2-26 (Apr–Jun 2026), from each company’s 10-Q.

### Shopify (Nasdaq / TSX: SHOP)

- **Founded:** 2006 in Ottawa by Tobi Lütke and Scott Lake. It began in
  2004 as Snowdevil, an online snowboard shop. Lütke built his own store
  software because nothing on the market worked, then sold the software
  instead of the snowboards.
- **Founder still in charge?** Yes. Lütke has been CEO since 2008. A
  special Founder Share gives him **40.02%** of total voting power, with
  a floor of 40% ([2026
  circular](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001594805/000159480526000022/yearend2025mic.htm)).
- **Listed:** May 2015 on the NYSE and TSX. The US listing now trades on
  Nasdaq.
- **Scale:** \$115.6bn of GMV in Q2-26, +32%. Shopify says its merchants
  captured nearly half of all incremental US e-commerce dollars since
  the start of 2025 (citing eMarketer).
- **What it sells:** the software a merchant runs on (Subscription
  Solutions) and the money flows through it, mainly Shopify Payments
  (Merchant Solutions).

| Revenue line                                 | Q2-26 share |
|----------------------------------------------|-------------|
| Merchant Solutions (payments, lending, fees) | 78%         |
| Subscription Solutions (plans, apps)         | 22%         |

- **CEO pay (2025):** \$35.0m, almost all in stock options. Salary of
  C\$1. President Harley Finkelstein: \$7.5m.

### Meta Platforms (Nasdaq: META)

- **Founded:** February 2004 at Harvard by Mark Zuckerberg, with Eduardo
  Saverin, Dustin Moskovitz, Chris Hughes and Andrew McCollum. Renamed
  from Facebook to Meta in 2021.
- **Founder still in charge?** Yes. Zuckerberg is chair and CEO and
  controls a majority of the vote through Class B shares, which makes
  Meta a “controlled company” under Nasdaq rules ([2026
  proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm)).
- **Listed:** 18 May 2012 on Nasdaq at \$38.
- **Scale:** apps used by about 3.6bn people (Facebook, Instagram,
  WhatsApp, Messenger, Threads).

| Revenue line                                   | Q2-26     | Share |
|------------------------------------------------|-----------|-------|
| Advertising                                    | \$59.36bn | 97.6% |
| Other (WhatsApp paid messaging, subscriptions) | \$1.01bn  | 1.7%  |
| Reality Labs (Quest, AI glasses)               | \$0.43bn  | 0.7%  |

- **CEO pay:** \$1 salary, no bonus, no equity awards. Reported pay is
  almost all security costs: \$27.2m in 2024
  ([MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/META-PLATFORMS-INC-10547141/news/Meta-CEO-Zuckerberg-s-Compensation-Totaled-27-2-Million-in-2024-49652046/)).
  His wealth sits in his shares.

### Amazon (Nasdaq: AMZN)

- **Founded:** July 1994 in Bellevue, Washington by Jeff Bezos, as an
  online bookstore. AWS launched in 2006.
- **Founder still in charge?** Partly. Bezos handed the CEO role to Andy
  Jassy in July 2021 and is Executive Chair. Jassy built AWS from its
  start.
- **Listed:** 15 May 1997 on Nasdaq at \$18.
- **Scale:** \$200.6bn of quarterly revenue and \$27.5bn of operating
  income in Q2-26. AWS made 60% of that operating income.

- **CEO pay (2025):** reported \$2.1m (salary \$365k plus about \$1.7m

A scenario is only useful if it can fail. These are the dated checks.
